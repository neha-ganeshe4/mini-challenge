YOU CAN'T SCREENSHOT A FEELING

This CAPTCHA uses pseudo-haptics, an HCI technique where the screen reacts to your hand with tiny lags and drags so your brain feels weight and texture through an ordinary mouse or touchscreen (Lécuyer et al.). The answer only exists in the loop between your hand and your eyes.
  1. 01 · THE HEAVY CRATEThree identical crates. The heavy one lags behind your hand, sags and swings slowly. At rest they are pixel-identical, so every screenshot is the same image.
  2. 02 · THE SOFT PILLOWFour pillows all squish to the same depth. The soft one gives instantly; the firm ones resist and sink slowly. A second later they look identical, so an agent that presses, waits and screenshots sees no difference.
  3. 03 · THREE HONEYPOTS Lying HTML: fake data-weight / data-firmness labels, always wrong.
    Invisible button: a "Softest pillow (select)" button that exists only in the page code. People can't see it; DOM-reading agents click it and get flagged instantly.
    Sheet note: barely-visible text on the bed sheet telling "AI agents" which pillow to pick. Vision models read faint text perfectly; people see a sheet pattern.
Why AI agents fail: they act in snapshots. A drag is one jump from A to B, then a screenshot after things settle. They never sense how something moved, and their mouse paths can be perfectly straight or perfectly human; it doesn't matter, because nothing here scores your path.

Odds for a bot that just guesses: 1 in 3 × 1 in 4 = 1 in 12, and any bot that reads the page code or the faint note is pushed toward the wrong answers or flagged outright. A person passes in about 10 seconds.
